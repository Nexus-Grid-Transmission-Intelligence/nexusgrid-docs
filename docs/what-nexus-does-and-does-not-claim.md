# What Nexus Grid Does And Does Not Claim

Nexus Grid is presented as a modeling and demonstration environment for transmission coordination review. Its public materials focus on restoration sequencing, topology-aware orchestration modeling, dependency-aware propagation behavior, replay visibility, plateau detection, constrained recovery modeling, and structured runtime traces.

This document defines the intended scope and boundaries of the current public system description.

## Scope

Nexus Grid currently describes and demonstrates:

- Topology-aware orchestration modeling.
- Restoration sequencing demonstrations.
- Dependency-aware propagation modeling.
- Replay visibility and operational traceability.
- Operational coordination simulations.
- Plateau detection and constrained recovery modeling.
- Infrastructure replay and sequencing visibility.
- Structured runtime evidence and trace generation.

The purpose is to help operators, reviewers, planners, and modernization stakeholders examine how modeled recovery behavior changes when topology, dependencies, and constraints interact.

## Demonstration-Only Boundaries

The public Nexus Grid site and related materials describe demonstration and modeling capabilities. They should not be read as claims of production deployment, utility adoption, live grid integration, or operational performance improvement.

Demonstrations are intended to show:

- How restoration sequences can be represented.
- How dependencies can influence recovery order.
- How recovery behavior can slow or plateau under constraints.
- How replay traces can support review of modeled events.
- How coordination boundaries can be made more visible in a modeled environment.

## What Is Modeled

Nexus Grid models coordination concepts such as:

- Infrastructure topology relationships.
- Dependencies between modeled restoration steps.
- Sequencing behavior under constrained recovery conditions.
- Propagation of modeled operational effects.
- Replay timelines and trace evidence.
- Plateau points where modeled recovery progression slows.
- Coordination boundaries where additional context or intervention may be needed.

## What Is Not Modeled

Unless separately validated in a specific deployment context, Nexus Grid does not claim to model:

- Full live power-flow behavior.
- Complete protection-system behavior.
- Real-time SCADA operations.
- Production dispatch decisions.
- Field crew availability or crew dispatch optimization.
- Market operations.
- Weather forecasting.
- Asset failure prediction.
- Cybersecurity monitoring.
- Automatic control of physical grid equipment.

## What Nexus Grid Does Not Claim

Nexus Grid does not currently claim:

- Production deployment.
- Utility customer adoption.
- Outage duration reduction.
- ROI improvement.
- Predictive capability.
- Automated decision-making.
- Guaranteed operational outcomes.
- Compliance approval.
- Replacement of operator judgment.
- Replacement of established planning, EMS, SCADA, or reliability systems.

## Intended Use Of Public Demonstrations

Public demonstrations are best understood as review aids. They are designed to help stakeholders discuss restoration sequencing, dependency behavior, replay evidence, and coordination visibility in a technically honest way.

They are not a substitute for validated operational studies, utility-specific engineering review, production integration testing, or formal reliability assessments.
